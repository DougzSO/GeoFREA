\section{Core Cost Parameters (Stage F6)}
\label{sec:params_group2}

\subsection{Scope and Conventions}
\label{subsec:g2_scope}

This section fixes the capital cost, fixed operating cost, discount rate,
lifetime and reference commissioning year used in the LCOE of Stage F6
(Equation~\ref{eq:theory_lcoe}). Grid, substation and road costs and
degradation belong to Group 3.

Currency and price year. All costs are in constant 2024 USD. The main
source, IRENA's report on plants commissioned in 2024, expresses all
installed costs and LCOE in 2024 USD \cite{irena2025costs2024}; no
deflator is needed. Values from the report on 2025 \cite{irena2026costs2025}
are in 2025 USD and are used only as context.

Reference year. The technology vintage is $t_{\mathrm{ref}}=2026$
(OQ-018). No observed cost cohort exists for 2026. The 2024 cohort is
used as the cost of the 2026 vintage, a transfer in time justified by
IRENA's finding that solar PV and onshore wind costs stabilized between
2024 and 2025 and that installed costs faced upward pressure into 2026
\cite{irena2026costs2025}. The transfer is recorded as a limitation.

Electrical basis. IRENA reports PV total installed costs per
kW$_{\mathrm{dc}}$ \cite{irena2025costs2024}, the basis used by GeoFREA,
so no conversion is applied. Stage F5 does not model AC output, and the
inverter loading ratio does not enter any calculation.

Sampling. Parameters sampled in Stage F6 (U-03) use uniform
distributions. The central value is the one used in deterministic runs.

\subsection{Capital Cost (OQ-017)}
\label{subsec:g2_capex}

Definition: total installed cost per unit of capacity at commissioning.
Unit: USD$_{2024}$/kW$_{\mathrm{dc}}$ (PV), USD$_{2024}$/kW (wind).
Enters: Equation~\ref{eq:theory_lcoe}; sampled in Stage F6.

IRENA reports, for each country and technology, the capacity-weighted
average and the 5th and 95th percentiles of project costs
\cite{irena2025costs2024}. Brazil and India have country values;
Portugal uses the European regional values. A regional value of the same
quantity and technology is a Tier 2 transfer, not a proxy.

The project distribution is right-skewed, so a uniform distribution on
the 5th--95th percentile interval would have a mean well above the
reported average and would raise every sampled LCOE. The sampling
interval is instead centred on the weighted average, with half-width
equal to the distance between the average and the 5th percentile:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{CAPEX} \sim \mathcal{U}\!\left[P_5,\; 2\bar{c}-P_5\right],
    \label{eq:g2_capex_interval}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{c}$ is the weighted average. The mean of the draws equals
the reported average. The upper tail beyond $2\bar{c}-P_5$ is excluded;
it reflects small, remote or complex projects, part of which GeoFREA
represents explicitly through its grid and road costs (Group 3).

\subsection{Fixed Operating Cost (OQ-016)}
\label{subsec:g2_opex}

Definition: annual fixed operation and maintenance cost as a fraction of
the capital cost (opex\_fixed\_frac). Unit: 1/yr. Enters:
Equation~\ref{eq:theory_lcoe} as $F=f\cdot I$; sampled in Stage F6.
Variable operating cost is set to zero, following the all-in fixed
convention of the cost sources.

Source values in USD/kW/yr are divided by the weighted-average capital
cost of the same country and technology, on the same capacity basis. For
PV in Brazil and India the working range is 6.99--11.0~USD/kW/yr,
extended to the IRENA global all-in value of 13.1~USD/kW/yr to cover the
difference between contract prices and all-in costs
\cite{irena2025costs2024}. For wind, single country values (Brazil 29,
India 23~USD/kW/yr) receive a $\pm20\%$ range by author judgment.
IRENA's O\&M table has no Portuguese row for wind; Portugal uses the
range of the European country values in that table.

\subsection{Discount Rate (OQ-022, OQ-054)}
\label{subsec:g2_wacc}

Definition: real after-tax weighted average cost of capital. Unit: 1/yr.
Enters: Equation~\ref{eq:theory_lcoe}; sampled in Stage F6.

Status: open. The only country- and technology-specific source checked so
far, IRENA's study of the cost of financing \cite{irena2023financing},
reports nominal after-tax rates for 2019--2021: for PV, 7.6\% in Brazil,
7.1\% in India and 10\% in Portugal; for wind, 6.8\% in Brazil and 8.4\%
in India; no Portuguese wind value was found. These values have three
problems for a 2026 vintage. They are nominal and require conversion with
Equation~\ref{eq:theory_fisher}. They describe a period of exceptionally
low interest rates, before the tightening that followed 2022, so they
would understate the cost of capital of a plant financed in 2026. And the
Portuguese PV value is out of line with the European context and with
the other rows, which suggests a reading or labeling error.
% verify: IRENA (2023) values were read from map labels; data annex not accessed

The value is selected by the following rule, fixed before any Stage F6
result. First choice: the discount rates IRENA uses in its LCOE for the
2024 cohort, if the report documents them by country and technology,
because they belong to the same cohort as the capital costs and
reproduce the LCOE benchmarks used for plausibility. Second choice: the
data annex of \cite{irena2023financing}. In both cases nominal rates are
converted with the central-bank inflation target in force in the years
the rates refer to: Banco Central do Brasil, Reserve Bank of India and
European Central Bank. Where a source gives a single value, the sampling
range is $\pm1$ percentage point by author judgment (Tier 3).

OQ-022 remains open until this rule is applied. It does not block
Stage F5; Stage F6 is also waiting for Group 3.

\subsection{Lifetime (OQ-018)}
\label{subsec:g2_lifetime}

Definition: economic lifetime $n$. Unit: yr. Enters:
Equation~\ref{eq:theory_lcoe}; sampled in Stage F6.

Central value 30 years for both technologies, the technical lifetime in
NREL's utility-scale PV and land-based wind assumptions
\cite{nrelATB2024definitions}. Sampling range 25--30 years, uniform. The
lower bound represents shorter economic lives (contract tenor, repowering)
and is author judgment. The central value sits at the upper end of the
range, so the uncertainty is one-sided by design.

Tier 2 (value), Tier 3 (range). Proxy: no.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:g2_limitations}

Costs of the 2024 cohort stand for the 2026 vintage. Portugal uses
European regional costs. The truncated upper tail of the capital-cost
distribution removes the most expensive projects, which is consistent
only if Group 3 represents the connection costs that drive them. Some
ranges are author judgment ($\pm20\%$ for single O\&M values). Treating
O\&M as a fraction of capital cost makes absolute O\&M scale with sampled
capital cost. The discount rate is not yet fixed.
