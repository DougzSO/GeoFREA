## 0. Summary

1. **CRAEI offers nothing for F6, F7 or F7b.** Of the 14 logics that the provenance map or the playbook attribute to CRAEI (LCOE kernel, Latin hypercube, streaming, feasibility, regret, max and P90, satisficing, ranking, variance decomposition, H1 to H4, PRIM, Kendall, the four F7b metrics), **none exists** in CRAEI's code, tests, configuration, documentation or git history (section 1). CRAEI is a heat and drought exposure framework for thermal and hydro plants; it has no cost model and no decision metric. The playbook lines "CRAEI has the LCOE kernel / LHS / regret / PRIM / Kendall / enrichment ratio" are **false**, as the provenance map's addendum of 2026-09-28 already suspected for the other rows. Verdict for every row: *not used*; four small CRAEI functions are consult-only (section 1.3). No A-11 header will be needed in F6, F7 or F7b: they are built fresh.
2. **F6 is blocked on values, not on design.** `parameters.json` has no `range` for any uncertain parameter of any real country, and no entry at all for `degradation_rate`, `grid_cost_usd_per_mw_km`, `substation_cost_usd_per_mw` and `road_cost_usd_per_km` (not even for ZZZ). The existing LHS sampler therefore raises for BRA, PRT and IND today. The blocks are OQ-001, OQ-016 to OQ-019, OQ-022 (F6) and the F5 blocks OQ-004, OQ-005, OQ-023 (section 2.4).
3. **Four findings that change the text or the data** (section 2.5): `opex_variable_usd_per_kwh` carries the unit USD/MWh and M-F6-01 calls it `opex_var_usd_per_mwh`; M-F7-06's secondary statistic cannot be computed from the F6 summaries as M-F6-04 defines them; the set of samples over which P90, mean and variance are taken (with or without the nominal vector) is not stated; and the IRENA total installed cost that `capex_usd_per_kw` cites may already include grid connection, which M-F6-01 adds again (to verify, section 2.5 item 4).
4. **Design in one paragraph** (section 3): a pure kernel that factors into a rank-3 numerator and a per-sample denominator, so the cell-by-sample block is one matrix product and three in-place passes; a per-member loop (the member axis is the outer one, as in F5); cells processed in chunks with **all** samples in memory, so the per-cell quantiles are exact and no sample-level array is ever persisted; energy of a sample from the stored F5 energy through `rescale_cf_*`, dispatched by the registry's `cf_model` so no technology name enters F6; outputs `lcoe_summary_<tech>.parquet`, `design_matrix_<tech>.parquet`, `supply_curve_<tech>.parquet`.
5. **Recommended F6 to F7 interface: (a)** F7 recomputes LCOE from the importable kernel and persists nothing sample-level (section 4). The reference level `L*` depends on `CF_min` (OQ-008), `q_ref` (OQ-020) and the F7 cell set, none of which F6 may know, so F6 cannot persist it. Estimated cost for BRA wind (181,277 central candidates, 172,250 in the F7 set, 19 core members): about 2 minutes per pass at 500 samples and about 16 minutes at 4,000 samples; peak memory below 1 GB at either size (section 4.4, measured throughput).
6. **16 decisions** for the author's verdict in section 5, each with options and a recommendation.

### 2.5 Findings of the inventory (to fix at implementation or by METHODOLOGY line)

1. **Name and unit of variable O&M.** `parameters.json` and `core/schemas.py` call it `opex_variable_usd_per_kwh` and give it the unit string "USD/MWh". M-F6-01 writes `opex_var_usd_per_mwh`. A name that says kWh with a unit that says MWh is an invitation to a factor-1000 error in a term that is zero today. Decision D9.
2. **M-F7-06 secondary statistic does not follow from M-F6-04.** The law of total variance is "computed from F6 summaries on the relative LCOE `X = LCOE / L*_f`". F6 summarizes absolute LCOE per (cell, member) over samples. `L*_f` changes with the sample (`f = (m, s)`), so `Var_s(LCOE / L*_(m,s))` is not a function of the mean and variance of `LCOE`. The relative-LCOE moments have to be accumulated by F7 in its own pass (section 4). This needs a wording fix of M-F7-06 (a PATCH, with authorization), not an F6 change.
3. **Which samples enter the summary.** M-F6-04 says "over samples" and M-F6-02 makes the nominal vector sample 0 of the design. Including sample 0 in the mean, variance and quantiles weights the nominal point once more than any draw; M-F7-03's "P90 over parameter samples" has the same ambiguity. Decision D3.
4. **Possible double counting of connection cost (to verify, not asserted).** `capex_usd_per_kw` cites IRENA's total installed cost (for PRT: "IRENA 2025, Table 3.1, Europe weighted-average TIC"). If the IRENA definition of total installed cost includes grid connection, then `P x dist_grid_km x grid_cost` plus `substation_cost` adds a cost that the CAPEX already contains for part of the connection. I did not read the IRENA report. The author should check the definition before OQ-001 closes; the answer decides whether the connection terms are an increment or a replacement.
5. **Every uncertain parameter is one value per country.** A sample `s` is country-wide: the same CAPEX in every cell. That is the consequence of M-F6-02 and of L-012, and it makes the cell ranking within a country depend on the parameters only through the cell-specific terms (distances, energy, capacity). Not a defect, but the thesis should state it; L-012 covers independence between parameters, not the perfect spatial correlation inside a country. Proposed addition to LIMITATIONS if the author agrees.
6. **`lifetime_years` is an integer in the kernel and a continuous variable in the sampler.** Decision D10.
7. **The ZZZ fixture cannot run F6**: four keys and all ranges are absent (D14).
8. **The F6 phase record is stale in the same way the F5 one was**: it requires `potential_<tech>.parquet` and `candidates_<tech>.parquet` (now `__<scenario>`), omits `forcing.parquet` (needed for `dT`) and `members.yaml`.

## 3. Proposed design

### 3.1 Principles

- **Pure functions in, tables out**, as in F5. Each formula is a vectorized NumPy function that cites its item (A-13); a thin pipeline reads parquet, checks the contract, calls the functions, writes parquet. No technology name appears in F6 (A-04): the registry's `cf_model` string selects how energy is rescaled (3.3).
- **The kernel is importable by F7 and does no I/O** (M-F6-05). It takes arrays and returns arrays; F7 owns its own loops.
- **Fail loud** (A-09): missing parameter, missing range, a non-finite result, an input outside its sanity range raise a named error before any output is written, with every missing item listed (same pattern as `MissingParameterError` of F5).
- **Determinism** (A-12): the design matrix is a function of (specification, N, seed); every member sees the same design, so futures `(m, s)` are paired across members.

### 3.2 The kernel, derived once so that it is small and fast

Write, for sample `s` (country-wide scalars) and cell `c` (the member is fixed by the call):

- `n = lifetime`, `r = discount rate`, `d = degradation`, `cap = capex_usd_per_kw`, `of = opex_fixed_frac`, `ov = opex_var_usd_per_mwh`, `g, sub, rd` = grid, substation and road costs.
- Annuity of a constant OPEX: `A_o(s) = (1 - (1+r)^(-n)) / r`.
- Energy factor with degradation: `A_e(s) = sum_{t=1..n} (1-d)^(t-1) / (1+r)^t = (1 - q^n) / ((1 - q)(1 + r))` with `q = (1-d)/(1+r)`; if `q = 1` (only when `r = d = 0`) the sum is `n`.
- Then `sum OPEX_t/(1+r)^t = of * P*1000*cap * A_o + ov * E_c * A_e`, and the variable term cancels against the denominator:

  **`LCOE(c, s) = ov_s + [ P_c * U_s + (P_c * dist_grid_c) * g_s + dist_road_c * rd_s ] / ( E_c(s) * A_e(s) )`, with `U_s = 1000 * cap_s * (1 + of_s * A_o(s)) + sub_s`.**

  The numerator is a product of a (cells x 3) matrix and a (3 x samples) matrix, a single BLAS call. `E_c(s)` is the stored F5 energy times a rescale factor: for wind `eta_s / eta_nominal`, a scalar per sample, so the denominator is `E_c` times a per-sample vector; for solar `(1 + gamma_s dT_c) / (1 + gamma_nominal dT_c)`, which is a rank-2 matrix `a_c + b_c gamma_s`. The result of `E_c(s) = 0` is a division by zero, handled by D7. `r = 0` makes `A_o` a 0/0; the kernel either uses the limit `n` or refuses `r <= 0` (D10).
- Closed forms for V-02: `d = 0`, constant OPEX and `ov = 0` give `LCOE = (CAPEX*CRF + OPEX) / E` with `CRF = r / (1 - (1+r)^(-n))`; `r -> 0` gives `(CAPEX + n*OPEX) / (n*E)`.

The factored form is the reason the time estimates of section 4.4 are small: it removes about ten temporary arrays of the textbook form.

### 3.3 Energy of a sample

`E_c,m(s) = E_MWh[c, m] * rho(c, m, s)`, with `rho` from `rescale_cf_*`. F6 needs the technology-specific choice (solar by `gamma` and `dT`, wind by `eta_loss`) without naming a technology. F5's D8 exported two free functions; picking one still needs a switch. Proposal: **the `CfModel` of F5's registry gains a method `rescale`** (same registry string, `cf_model`) that returns the factor for the sample vector, so F6 calls `get_cf_model(cfg.cf_model).rescale(...)`. A small change in F5, tested there. Alternative: a second registry string `energy_rescale` in `technologies.yaml`. Decision D12.

`rho = 1` at the nominal vector, so `LCOE(c, m, s0)` must equal the LCOE computed directly from the F5 table; V-03 tests it.

### 3.4 Loop and memory (A-10, M-F6-04)

For each member `m` (outer loop, as in F5), for each **chunk of cells** (inner loop), with **all `N+1` samples** in memory:

1. Build the (cells x 3) and (3 x samples) factors, one matrix product, divide in place, add `ov` in place: the (cells x samples) block `L`.
2. From `L`: the nominal LCOE is column 0; mean and variance over the draws; `p10, p50, p90` by `numpy.quantile` along the sample axis (exact); the count of non-finite values (D7).
3. Append the chunk's rows to the member's output; the block is freed.

The block is transient: no sample-level array is persisted (M-F6-04). Chunk size from the budget: `cells_per_chunk = floor(max_batch_gb * 1e9 / (4 * 8 * (N + 1)))`, where 4 counts the live (cells x samples) arrays at the peak. Exact quantiles need all samples of a cell at once, which is why the chunk axis is the cell axis. **This differs from the literal "samples are processed in batches" of M-F6-04**: the outcome that clause asks for (bounded memory, no persisted sample array) is met, but the batching axis is cells. If the author prefers batching along samples, the summaries become approximate (D2).

Writers append row groups per member (`pyarrow.ParquetWriter`), so the table is never held whole.

## 4. Interface F6 to F7

### 4.1 What F7 needs

For each cell `i` and future `f = (m, s)`: `r_i,f = (LCOE_i,f - L*_f) / L*_f`, where `L*_f` is the `q_ref` quantile of LCOE over the **feasible** cells of `f` (M-F7-02). Then `MR_i = max_m P90_s r_i,(m,s)`. Feasibility is `CF(m, s) >= CF_min` with `CF(m, s)` from `rescale_cf_*`. `L*_f` depends on all cells of one future. A cell infeasible in a sample takes the regret of the feasible cell with the highest LCOE in `f`. The secondary statistic needs, per (cell, member), the mean and variance over `s` of `LCOE / L*_f`.

### 4.2 Options

| | (a) F7 recomputes through the kernel | (b) F6 persists `L*` per future and a streaming quantile | (c) F6 persists the sample-level LCOE |
|---|---|---|---|
| What is persisted | nothing sample-level; F6's `lcoe_summary` and `design_matrix` only | a small table `(member, sample, L*, n_feasible)` plus P90 of `r` per (cell, member) | the full (cell, member, sample) array |
| Depends on `CF_min` (OQ-008), `q_ref` (OQ-020), the F7 cell set | no: F7 reads them when it runs | **yes**: F6 would have to read F7's parameters, and rerun when they change | no |
| Size for BRA wind, N = 500 | 0 extra | tiny table, but wrong after any change of `CF_min` or `q_ref` | 172,250 x 19 x 501 x 8 B = 13 GB per core window (float64); 105 GB at N = 4,000 |
| Kernel evaluations | 2 per (member, sample, cell) | 1 | 1, plus I/O of 13 GB or more |
| Coupling | F7 imports F6's kernel (already M-F6-05) | F6 imports F7's thresholds: the DAG runs backward | none, but a violation of M-F6-04 |
| Verdict | **recommended** | not recommended | rejected (M-F6-04 forbids it) |

### 4.3 Recommendation: (a), in two passes per member

F7, per member `m`:

- **Pass 1** (per sample, across cells): `L*_(m,s)` and the highest feasible LCOE of the future. With `q_ref = 0` this is a running minimum over cell chunks (no batching problem). If `q_ref > 0` the quantile over cells for each sample needs all cells of a sample, so pass 1 batches along **samples** (this is the use of "samples in batches" that M-F6-04 describes). A small vector of length N+1 per member survives pass 1.
- **Pass 2** (per cell chunk, all samples): kernel again, `r`, P90 over samples, update of the running maximum over members, the SR counters, and the running mean and variance of `LCOE / L*_f` for M-F7-06.

State kept across members: `MR` (cells), SR counters (cells), per-member mean and variance of `X` (cells x members, 172,250 x 19 x 2 x 8 B = 52 MB). Nothing else. The convergence of the sample size (U-04) is run by a driver that calls F6's sampler and F7's `MR` function for each size, which is a circularity of the method (D4).

## 5. Decisions for the author's verdict

**D1. F6 to F7 interface.** Options: (a) F7 recomputes through the kernel; (b) F6 persists `L*` per future and a streaming quantile; (c) another, such as persisting the sample-level array. Recommendation: **(a)**, section 4. (b) makes F6 depend on `CF_min`, `q_ref` and the F7 set, and the persisted `L*` is wrong after any change of those; (c) is forbidden by M-F6-04 and costs 13 to 105 GB.

**D2. Quantiles: exact per cell chunk, or streaming.** Options: (a) cells in chunks, all samples in memory, exact `numpy.quantile`; (b) samples in batches with a streaming estimator (P-square, t-digest) for p10, p50, p90, Welford for mean and variance; (c) exact quantiles by a two-pass histogram. Recommendation: **(a)**. It is exact, the memory bound is the same, the cost is the 62,000-to-7,800-cell chunk, and it needs no new dependency or approximation error to report. It departs from the literal wording "samples are processed in batches" of M-F6-04; a one-line PATCH of that item says "in chunks" (needs the author's authorization). With (b) the p90 carries an estimator error that would propagate into `MR`.

**D3. Definitions of the summaries.** (i) Quantile method: `numpy.quantile` default (linear interpolation, Hyndman-Fan type 7) vs another type. (ii) Variance: sample (`ddof = 1`) vs population. (iii) Samples included: the `N` draws only, with the nominal vector reported separately, vs all `N + 1`. Recommendation: type 7 (also what CRAEI's `p90_threshold` uses), `ddof = 1`, **draws only**, nominal separate. The same set (draws only) for F7's P90 over samples; the nominal future `f0` stays in the regret, the feasibility and the climate-only comparison. Needs a METHODOLOGY clarification of M-F6-04 and M-F7-03 (PATCH).

**D4. Initial sample size and the U-04 protocol.** `experiments.yaml` already has `initial_size: 500`, and U-04 says double until the Jaccard of top-k by `MR` between consecutive sizes changes by less than 0.01 for all countries and technologies. Open points: (i) `MR` is an F7 output, so the protocol cannot live in F6 (F6 cannot import F7); (ii) top-k needs `p_k` (OQ-021); (iii) a ceiling for the doubling is needed so that a non-converging case stops (`max_size_for_convergence` is commented out in `experiments.yaml`); (iv) the draws of sizes 500 and 1,000 are independent, not nested, so the Jaccard between them includes sampling noise (acceptable, state it). Recommendation: keep 500; implement the protocol as a driver outside both phases (a script plus a recorded result, the adopted size written to `experiments.yaml` `sampler`), run after F7 exists; ceiling and `p_k` are the author's values (OQ-021 and a new number). Until then F6 runs at 500.

**D5. `memory.max_batch_gb`.** The setting does not exist. Options for the value: 0.5, 1, 2 GB. The machine has 5.9 GB total. Recommendation: **1 GB** as the proposed value for the author to confirm or change, a new `memory` section in `settings.yaml`, the chunk size computed from it (3.4) and logged. The value is the author's.

**D6. Default distribution per parameter.** `UncertainSpec` supports uniform and triangular with the mode at the nominal. Recommendation as a rule, applied when the range source is filled in: **uniform** when the source gives only a minimum and a maximum, **triangular** only when the source states a most likely value that equals the stored nominal. Provisional reading per parameter (to confirm when each range is sourced): `capex_usd_per_kw`, `discount_rate`, `gamma`, `eta_loss` are often reported with a central estimate (triangular candidates); `opex_fixed_frac`, `lifetime_years`, `degradation_rate`, `grid_cost_usd_per_mw_km`, `substation_cost_usd_per_mw`, `road_cost_usd_per_km` usually come as intervals (uniform). A triangular distribution whose mode is not the source's central estimate would invent information. The field `range.distribution` is per parameter and country, so no default is written in code.

**D7. LCOE with zero energy.** Cases: `E = 0` (a capacity factor of exactly zero, or a rescale factor of zero). Options: (a) the kernel returns `+inf`, the summary keeps the order statistics (p10, p50, p90 are well defined with `+inf`), mean and variance are computed over the finite samples, and `n_nonfinite` is persisted; (b) NaN; (c) raise. Recommendation: **(a)**. An infinite LCOE is the correct economic statement (no energy, finite cost), it orders correctly, and F7 handles it as infeasible (`CF < CF_min`) before it divides. (c) would stop a whole country for one cell. A negative or non-finite energy, or a non-finite cost term, still raises (A-09).

**D8. Currency and base year (S-07).** `parameters.json` entries have `unit: "USD/kW"` and a source, but no price year; sources are IRENA 2024/2025 and others. Options: (a) add an optional `price_year` to the U-05 entry and make F6 raise if a cost parameter has none or differs from 2024; (b) write the year in the `unit` string and test it; (c) a deflator table in configuration. Recommendation: **(a)**, no deflator code now (there is no source in another base year to convert; (c) would be code for no input). The author audits, per cost parameter of PRT, BRA and IND, the price year of the cited source; I did not. Real versus nominal discount rate: M-F6-01 is in real terms, the stored `discount_rate` comes from IRENA's cost-of-capital benchmark, and the author should confirm that the cited value is a real rate.

**D9. Names and unread keys.** (i) Rename `opex_variable_usd_per_kwh` to `opex_var_usd_per_mwh` (key, schema, ZZZ), the name M-F6-01 already uses; no METHODOLOGY change. (ii) F6 does not read `discount_rate_increment` (its own note says `discount_rate` is final; M-F6-01 has no premium term). Recommendation: do (i) in the first F6 commit with a test that the unit and the name agree; leave (ii) unread and say so in the F6 record. Alternative to (i): keep the key and change METHODOLOGY (worse: the stored unit is MWh).

**D10. Lifetime as a sampled integer and degenerate rates.** `lifetime_years` is sampled continuously, the kernel sums integer years. Options: (a) round the sampled value to the nearest integer year in the design matrix, so the stored design is what the kernel used; (b) accept a real `n` in the closed form (a continuous extension that is not a physical sum); (c) sample a discrete uniform. Recommendation: **(a)**. For rates, refuse `discount_rate <= 0` and `degradation_rate` outside `[0, 1)` at range validation (the kernel's `r = 0` branch exists only for the V-02 limit test); the author's ranges (OQ-022, OQ-019) are expected positive.

**D11. Which members and windows F6 covers.** Options: (a) all 37 members, including the 18 of 2071-2100; (b) the core window and `m0` only. Recommendation: **(a)**. M-F7-07 H1 repeats on the sensitivity window, F6 cost is about 2x, and the cell-members masked by M-F4-07 simply have no row. F7's set uses only the core window (D-F7-005).

**D12. Where the technology-specific energy rescale lives.** Options: (a) a `rescale` method on F5's `CfModel`, selected by the existing `cf_model` string; (b) a second registry string `energy_rescale`; (c) F6 imports `rescale_cf_wind` and `rescale_cf_solar` and branches. Recommendation: **(a)** (A-04: no technology name in F6; one registry key). It is a small additive change in F5 with its own test; no stored value changes.

**D13. Outputs: shape and names.** (i) `design_matrix_<tech>.parquet` per technology rather than the single `design_matrix.parquet` of M-F6-06 (a run has two technologies and the design depends on each technology's parameters); (ii) supply curve at cell resolution per member (`member, rank, cell_id, lcoe_nominal, cum_P_GW, cum_E_TWh`, up to 6.7 M rows for BRA wind) vs binned (for example 1% of potential per bin); (iii) float64 for the statistics. Recommendation: (i) yes, with a PATCH line for the file name; (ii) cell resolution, because F8 may not recompute (M-F8-01) and a cumulative sum is a computation; (iii) float64 (the table is a few hundred MB).

**D14. The synthetic country for F6 (A-06).** ZZZ needs the four missing keys (`degradation_rate`, `grid_cost_usd_per_mw_km`, `substation_cost_usd_per_mw`, `road_cost_usd_per_km`) and a range for every uncertain parameter, all `synthetic: true`, plus a price year. The values are test values the author checks in the diff, as for F5. Recommendation: do it in the first F6 commit; F6 runs on ZZZ from F1 to F6 in the existing synthetic test.

**D15. Phase, DAG and maps.** `lcoe_modeling` requires the F5 central potential tables, the F3 central candidates, `forcing` and `members`; `lcoe_maps` is a separate phase after the tables (as `potential_maps` was), COG and PNG of nominal, median, p10 and p90 LCOE at `m0` and a supply-curve figure, honouring `figures`. Not in `target_phases` until the real parameters exist, like F5. Recommendation: as written; tables first, maps in a second commit.

**D16. Registry changes.** `required_parameters` gains the non-uncertain F6 inputs (variable O&M); `uncertain_parameters` already lists the nine F6 and F5 ones. `--production` then refuses a missing value or range with the full list. Recommendation: as written; no new mechanism.
