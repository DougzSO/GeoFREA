# LIMITATIONS.md: GeoFREA

Declared limitations of the method and every Tier 3 value. Each entry is written so it can be carried into the thesis limitations section.

Entry format: ID, statement, affected items, why it is accepted, where it is declared in the thesis (filled when written).

## Method limitations

| ID | Statement | Affects | Why accepted | Thesis section |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L-001 | Solar potential assumes free-standing plants with c-Si modules at fixed optimum tilt (Global Solar Atlas PVOUT configuration); single-axis trackers are not modeled. | M-F5-02, T-R1, T-R2 | Only globally consistent PVOUT product available; trackers would require a separate irradiance model. | |
| L-002 | Climate change enters through annual-mean delta-change factors; seasonal redistribution of resource is not represented. | M-F4-03, M-F5-02, M-F5-03 | Atlas baselines are annual long-term averages; monthly baseline redistribution would require monthly atlas products and profiles. | |
| L-003 | Wind change factors from near-surface (10 m) `sfcWind` are applied at hub height, and Weibull shape `k` is held constant under climate change. | M-F5-03 | CMIP6 does not provide hub-height wind for the required ensemble; IPCC AR6 assigns low confidence to regional wind-speed trends, which is discussed alongside results. | |
| L-004 | The wind capacity factor is evaluated per 0.01 degree pixel and aggregated to the cell by the eligible area (D-F5-038), but the climate factors are not: `dT` and `delta_wind` of F4 are those of the GCM cell and are constant over the pixels of the analysis cell, so the change of the wind regime with the terrain inside a cell enters only through the reference climate. Solar keeps the cell mean (linear model, no loss). | M-F5-03, M-F4 | The GCM resolution (about 100 to 250 km) is far coarser than the cell; a finer factor would be an invented pattern. Before the pixel evaluation the capacity factor of 6% to 7% of the cells of IND and BRA was more than 1% off (`docs/phases/F5/wind_cf_pixel_74.md`); that part is removed. | |
| L-005 | Each GCM contributes a single realization; internal variability is not sampled. | M-F4-01 | Ensemble size is bounded by variable availability and computation; 30-year climatologies reduce internal-variability noise. | |
| L-006 | Climate of each window is held constant over the asset lifetime. | M-F6-01 | Annual trajectories add noise without changing results materially under discounting; two windows bracket the time dimension. | |
| L-007 | The 2071-2100 window extends beyond the lifetime of assets commissioned around 2030 and is interpreted as repowering or second-generation assets. | S-05, T-R6 | Needed to test H1 where SSPs diverge. | |
| L-008 | The reference climatology (1995-2014) does not coincide with the atlas reference periods (Global Solar Atlas: region-dependent start to 2024; Global Wind Atlas: see OQ-006). | M-F4-03 | Delta-change requires a common CMIP6 historical reference; 1995-2014 is the IPCC AR6 recent-past reference. | |
| L-009 | The transmission grid layer represents line location, not available connection capacity or voltage adequacy. | M-F2b-02, M-F6-01 | No consistent grid-capacity data exist for the three countries. | |
| L-010 | Exclusions E1 to E5 are shares of each pixel and E6 is binary; social acceptance and other non-monetizable siting factors enter only through exclusions. | M-F2b-01 | Keeps every output in physical units; the land-availability scenarios bound the effect (U-06). | |
| L-011 | Grid connection is costed per cell from eligible-area-weighted mean distance; shared connection infrastructure between neighboring cells is not modeled. | M-F6-01 | Cell decision unit (S-06); zones may be shown in F8 for presentation only. | |
| L-012 | Uncertain parameters are sampled independently; correlations (for example between CAPEX and discount rate) are not represented (verdict V5: independent sampling; no correlation without a sourced coefficient). | U-03, V5 | No defensible joint distributions for the three countries. | |
| L-013 | Results depend on the 0.05 degree aggregation (modifiable areal unit problem). | S-06, V-07 | Tested explicitly at 0.1 degree. | |
| L-014 | Extreme-wind context comes from ERA5 historical reanalysis and does not vary by SSP. | M-F1-05, M-F4-05, T-R10 | CMIP6 lacks consistent gust variables; low confidence in wind extremes trends. | |
| L-015 | Hazards without Tier 1-2 loss functions are reported as context and do not affect robustness metrics. | M-F4-05, H4 | Avoids driving results with unsourced values; exposure is still reported (T-R10). | |
| L-016 | External validation against existing plants measures plausibility, not accuracy, because plant locations reflect past auctions, policy, and grid access. | M-F7b-01 to M-F7b-04 | No ground truth for optimal siting exists. | |
| L-017 | The gust indicator (`fg10`, annual maximum, 1995-2014) is raw ERA5 reanalysis with no bias adjustment against station or other observed data. It is internally consistent for comparison across cells and years but is not an observed local gust value. | M-F1-05, M-F4-05, T-R10 | No bias-adjusted gust product exists at the required resolution/coverage across BRA, PRT, IND; CRAEI (which does bias-adjust its ISIMIP3b hazard channel, D-F4-001) has no gust product at all to adapt (D12). | |
| L-018 | The `grid` layer is OpenStreetMap power infrastructure of any voltage (lines and points), so distance to the grid is the distance to the nearest mapped feature, which can be a low-voltage line unable to absorb a utility-scale plant; the true connection point and the reinforcements it needs are not modeled. | M-F1-02, M-F2a-03, M-F3-03, M-F6-01 | No country-wide, voltage-resolved transmission dataset is available for BRA, PRT and IND; the effect is carried by the sampled grid-cost range (OQ-001). **Coverage measured 2026-10-06** (existing plants against the OSM layer, `docs/_audit/2026-10_plants_vs_osm_grid.md`): PRT complete (all plants within 14 km); BRA consistent (99.9% of capacity within 50 km; far distances are mostly real absence of grid); **IND incomplete** (24% of wind, 11% of solar, 41% of hydro units more than 50 km from a mapped feature; repeated 2026-10-07 with GEM, 5,727 IND units, same verdict as the 2020 GPPD list), so IND distances to the grid are overstated. | |
| L-019 | Connection CAPEX is linear in the distance to the nearest eligible substation (`c_line x tau x D_sub`, plus a bay and a road, per project over `P_ref`). Real costs also depend on voltage, line technology, capacity, terminal and reinforcement costs, losses and the actual route, and the cost-distance relation changes at long distances (HVDC vs HVAC), so the linear form is a first-order approximation, least reliable for remote cells. | M-F6-01, M-F6-08, M-F2a-02, OQ-040 | Evidence supports distance entering cost continuously but gives no per-country voltage/technology/route data; the sampled unit-cost ranges (OQ-001), the tortuosity range and the named sensitivities (M-F6-10) carry the uncertainty. See `docs/_audit/2026-10_distance_connection_cost_evidence.md`. | |
| L-048 | The connection cost is that of the radial spur, the works at the receiving substation and an access road. Upstream network reinforcement is not included and can dominate real interconnection costs, so the cost is a lower bound of a full interconnection-study estimate. | M-F6-08 | No country-wide reinforcement data; including it would invent values. The boundary is declared in the thesis (`subsec:g3_boundary`). | |
| L-049 | The point of interconnection comes from OSM tags, which are incomplete. The raw extracts held substations as points only (12, 4 and 3 eligible in BRA, IND and PRT), so the substations are read as nodes, ways and relations from Overpass (`scripts/fetch_osm_substations.py`, 2026-11-02): 3,892 (BRA), 3,239 (IND) and 313 (PRT) pass the voltage threshold (`docs/_audit/poi_counts_84.md`). Many substations have no voltage, neither from the tag nor from the lines, and are excluded (BRA 1,671 of 8,662, IND 6,439 of 17,056, PRT 12,471 of 16,109), so `D_sub` is an upper bound of the distance to the network wherever the voltage is missing; the Brazilian substation count (8,662) is low against Portugal (16,109) and is the figure most likely to reflect mapping gaps. The Overpass snapshot is dated in the sidecar of each file. | M-F2a-09, M-F6-08 | The source is a decision of the author; the gridfinder distance is kept as the optimistic sensitivity `poi_gridfinder_lines`. | |
| L-050 | Tortuosity is one uniform factor (1.25, range 1.2 to 1.3) for the line and for the road, not a routed path. | M-F6-08 | No routing data; the range is sampled (Tier 3, L-212). | |
| L-051 | The unit costs exclude right of way in India and come from a higher voltage class in Portugal (ACER 220 kV used at 150 kV, conservative). The IRENA capital-cost boundary (collection, collector substations, step-up transformers, switchyard) is stated for PV and assumed for onshore wind. | M-F6-08 | The only regulator-compiled benchmarks available; the assumption for wind is recorded here, not tested. | |
| L-052 | Degradation rates are transferred from United States fleets (PV: Jordan et al. 2022, the fleet has no system in T1 and T7, so T1 and T2 take the T3 rate and T7 the T6 rate in the central case; the sensitivity `pv_hot_zone_degradation` (0.94 %/yr, Straub and Mueck 2025) shows what the T6 rate hides: T7 or above holds 43.7 % of the eligible solar area of BRA, 81.6 % of IND and 0 % of PRT (`docs/_audit/pv_zone_shares_84.md`); wind: Hamilton et al. 2020, one reference vintage). The PV thermal zone is fixed at the historical climate of the PVCZ dataset and does not change with the climate member. The hot-zone rate rests on 50 systems. | M-F6-09 | No fleet data for the three countries; the zone rates are Tier 2 and the range runs from the median to the mean. | |
| L-056 | The BRA line cost is the ANEEL modular (reference) cost per km applied in three EPE studies, not an as-built investment; one of the three price bases (May 2019, two cases) is inferred from the size of the value. The IND bay is a proxy: a bank guarantee per terminal bay, not a capital cost. | M-F6-08, OQ-001 | The ANEEL authorizing resolutions could not be downloaded (HTTP 403); no bay cost was found for India. | |
| L-054 | The access road is not billed in any country in the central scenario (`road_cost_excluded`): no road cost with a source serves the three countries. The central LCOE is a lower bound; the sensitivity `road_cost_high` exists for BRA only (DNIT 2016, paved highway, an upper bound). | M-F6-10, OQ-001 | ROCKS covers low and middle income countries up to 2008 (not Portugal), PMGSY has no official unit cost, DNIT is a 2016 paved-highway benchmark (`docs/_audit/group3_costs_85.md`). | |
| L-055 | The BRA bay is the ANEEL BPR module Entrada de Linha 138 kV BPT: items carry their own reference index (mostly 6/2017, eight at 12/2011), so BRL 1.99 million is not one date; the ACER bay of PRT is a per-station indicator (substation with 0 to 5 bays, AIS upgrade), read as the works at the receiving substation. | M-F6-08, OQ-001 | The only published modular costs found; both are Tier 2 with status `verify`. | |
| L-053 | Transmission losses are not modelled in the central case (`eta_trans = 1`); only the sensitivity applies a linear loss of 0.03 to 0.05 %/km. | M-F6-10 | No country-specific line parameters; the loss rate is Tier 3 (L-213). | |
| L-020 | RETIRED 2026-11-05 (COMMAND 97): it described a candidate set that rested on a Tier 3 population-density value (200 persons/km2, OQ-047). The density threshold no longer excludes land: E6 is a setback around the GHS settlements (M-F2a-11, M-F2b-01), and the density is the named sensitivity `population_density_200`, not run. | | The entry is kept so the ID is not reused; the settlement limits are L-050. | |
| L-021 | Hazard exposure (T-R10 and the exposure half of H5) covers the nine members of the three GCMs with an ISIMIP3b counterpart (GFDL-ESM4, IPSL-CM6A-LR, MRI-ESM2-0) in the 2041-2070 window. The other three GCMs and the 2071-2100 window have no hazard rows (S-05, D-F4-004); they are reported as not available, never as zero, and no exposure statement is made for 2071-2100. | M-F4-05, M-F7-07 (H5), T-R10 | The 2071-2100 hazard data (about 183 GB) are not acquired before OQ-007 says that a hazard enters the LCOE (V8). | |
| L-022 | The scenario-discovery step uses a minimal own PRIM (peeling and pasting, real and categorical descriptors, one box). It reproduces `ema_workbench` 3.0.0 on five stored cases but differs in three documented ways: no integer descriptors (cast to real), categorical pastes are considered even when no real descriptor is restricted, and categories are visited in sorted order. The trajectory is written and the box is chosen by the pre-registered rule (the densest with coverage of at least 0.50 and at most three restricted descriptors, M-F7-08). The box describes the futures evaluated, not the space of possible futures; boxes are axis-aligned and describe poorly a vulnerability that depends on a ratio of variables; the climate descriptors are summaries over the shortlist and, with six climate models, a restricted GCM may reflect one model, not a physical condition. | M-F7-08 | `tests/fixtures/prim_reference.json` and `tests/unit/test_robustness_prim.py`; `scripts/generate_prim_reference.py` regenerates the fixture in a Python 3.12 environment outside `.venv`. | |
| L-023 | National expansion targets usually state the total installed capacity of a technology, including the plants already built, while the technical potential of GeoFREA does not remove the cells that existing plants occupy. The slack `H_0 = E_0 - G` of H5 is therefore a screening margin whose value depends on the source, the horizon and the unit conversion of the target. | M-F7-07 (H5), T-R12, OQ-010 | Removing existing plants from the potential needs plant locations and capacities that F7b uses only to validate plausibility (L-016); the source, the horizon and the original unit of each target are recorded with every result (`national_targets`), and alternative targets and conversions are sensitivities of H5. | |
| L-024 | The wind ensemble has five GCMs: the wind channel of MIROC6 is excluded in every country because its 1995-2014 mean 10 m wind over Brazil is below 0.5 m/s in 50.3% of its native land cells (minimum 0.001 m/s), against 0% in four of the other five GCMs and 18.8% in IPSL-CM6A-LR. The irradiance and temperature of MIROC6 are used, so solar has six GCMs. The other GCMs also have low 10 m winds over the Amazon, and the ratio of window to reference wind divides by them; the mask `wind_factor_valid_range` (no source, OQ-043) stays as a safeguard. | S-04, M-F4-02 item 6, M-F4-03, M-F4-07, T-R3, T-R6 | A reference field that is 0.11 of the Global Wind Atlas value (0.26 to 0.49 for the others) is not a basis for a ratio; excluding the channel acts on the cause, and the mask alone left 91,069 BRA cells that rest on a reference below 1 m/s (diagnostics report, section 2). | |
| L-025 | Wind candidates above the altitude limit of the scenario (`elevation_max_m` of `land_availability`: 2,000 m restrictive, 3,000 m central, none permissive; the central 3,000 m is Tier 3 with no source, the restrictive one follows siting studies, status verify) are not candidates in it; in the central scenario this affects India only (PRT and BRA have none; their highest wind candidates are 1356 m and 1922 m). In the F5 run of 2026-10-16 that did not exclude them (before OQ-060 and the contiguous mask; 61,464 central wind cells now) the cells were 1,316 of 62,718 central cells, 0.71 GW (0.37% of the capacity) and 1.11 TWh/yr (0.37% of the energy) in the central scenario; 8.3 GW in the permissive one. The limit has no source: a sourced criterion (IEC site conditions, turbine datasheets, density derating) is OQ-058, which stays open. | M-F2b-06, M-F3-04, M-F5-03, OQ-058 | A very high plateau is a siting question that a density correction does not settle; the cells are few, and the exclusion removes the cells whose air density is below 0.75 of the curve density from the central results. The density correction itself is applied at every altitude and is not truncated (group1_technical_potential subsec:g1_turbine). | |
| L-026 | `dT` is kept as the GCM gives it: three of the 36 members have cells with a negative `dT` over Brazil (6,264 cell-members, 0.06%), and the native cells of MRI-ESM2-0 that depart by more than 1 K from their neighbours are flagged (`dT_isolated_native_cell`) and counted, not corrected. The cause of the isolated cells is not diagnosed. | M-F4-03, M-F4-06, M-F5-02 | The effect on a solar cell is below 1% of its output per K of `dT`; a correction would be a rule with a free threshold. | |

| L-057 | The width of a river reach is estimated from its long-term mean discharge with the global hydraulic-geometry relation of Andreadis et al. (`W = 7.2 Q^b`, `b` 0.48 / 0.50 / 0.52), a bankfull relation applied to mean discharge, so `W` tends to underestimate the legal channel width: the corridor is biased to the permissive side. HydroRIVERS omits small streams and distinguishes neither ephemeral channels nor navigable waters; the 50 m margin of Portuguese estuaries is not represented. For a mean discharge below 1 m3/s `Q^b` falls as `b` grows, so the width of each reach is taken at the extreme of the exponent range (`W = 7.2 min(Q^0.48, Q^0.52)` permissive, `7.2 max(Q^0.48, Q^0.52)` restrictive, `7.2 Q^0.50` central) and the corridors of the scenarios are nested on every reach (decision of Douglas, 2026-11-05). | M-F2b-01 (E3), M-F3-02, U-06 | The relation is the published global estimate; the effect on the eligible area is far below the precision of the inputs. | group6_land_eligibility subsec:g6_riparian, subsec:g6_limitations |
| L-058 | The river corridor is merged with the land-cover water classes by union, but the land cover holds class counts and no geometry inside a pixel, so the union is taken as the corridor share net of the water share, `max(0, (c - w) / (1 - w))`: exact when the water lies inside the corridor, and permissive for a river wider than its estimated corridor (L-057). Lakes (E2) and the water class stay independent of each other, as before. | M-F2b-01 (E3), M-F3-01 | The overlap inside a pixel is not known; the alternatives were a double count (the channel counted twice) or a new 10 m water layer in F2a (decision C1, Douglas, 2026-11-05). | group6_land_eligibility subsec:g6_riparian |
